In July 2026, the EU Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) deferred the AI Act's high-risk dates. The easy read is "regulation just softened." The better read: **the preparation window got longer — and it was already open.**

## What's in force today

The AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, in force since 1 August 2024) has been phasing in for two years. Two obligations are already live:

- **Art. 4 — AI literacy** (since 2 Feb 2025): staff who use or manage AI systems need a baseline of competence.
- **Art. 50 — transparency** (since 2 Aug 2026): AI-generated content must be machine-readable marked as synthetic, and users of certain systems must know they're interacting with AI.

These apply *now*, to anyone deploying AI in the EU — including engineering teams using generative tools on their design floor.

## What's coming (the deferred dates)

The Digital Omnibus moved the high-risk obligations:

| Obligation | Original date | New date |
|---|---|---|
| Annex III standalone high-risk systems | 2 Aug 2026 | **2 Dec 2027** |
| Annex I embedded high-risk (machinery, medical) | 2 Aug 2027 | **2 Aug 2028** |

If your product is machinery or medical (Annex I) — which most OEM and Tier-1 products are, directly or in the supply chain — the high-risk regime lands on **you**: risk management, technical documentation, logging, human oversight. The deferral bought 12–24 months of runway. It did not cancel the destination.

## What this means for engineering teams

Three practical consequences:

1. **The documentation work is now a differentiator.** Teams that can *show* their AI design process is controlled, logged and auditable will win procurement conversations in 2027–2028 — not because the AI is smarter, but because it's provable.
2. **Minimal-risk doesn't mean no-risk.** A tool that is itself minimal-risk (like the Gym — it's neither an Annex I product nor in any Annex III domain) can still be *the* way you build the evidence for your high-risk downstream use.
3. **Provenance maps one-to-one.** The Act's requirements — logging, technical documentation, human oversight — are exactly what an audit trail provides: which prompt, which model, which validation results, whose sign-off.

## The bottom line

The window is open: **2 Dec 2027** for standalone high-risk, **2 Aug 2028** for embedded. That's not "someday" — it's the next two budget cycles. The teams that treat provenance as a product feature, not an afterthought, will be the ones regulators and customers trust when the dates arrive.
